# Fort–Pettah Gateway — Visual Reference Notes (text only)

These are written notes on the visual identity the slice represents. No photographs are stored in this repository; the brief does not permit copyrighted reference photos.

The geography is **compressed and fictionalised**. It aims to capture the character of the area, not a survey-accurate street map. Shop names are invented.

## Fort railway station
- A long, white, colonial-era railway station facing a busy road.
- Signature features:
  - a ground-floor arcade;
  - a central pavilion with a pediment and a small clock/tower element;
  - deep shade under the colonnade.
- Signage in English, Sinhala and Tamil: COLOMBO FORT / කොළඹ කොටුව / கொழும்பு கோட்டை.
- On the forecourt:
  - a tuk rank where three-wheelers wait in a line;
  - vendors and people with bags crossing toward the platforms.
- Behind the station:
  - platforms and carriages, glimpsed;
  - train horns and the clatter of rolling stock.

## Transport corridor (Olcott-style)
- A wide road with a median, heavy bus traffic, bus bays with shelters, and pedestrian railings along the kerb edge.
- Buses dominate the scale and the noise: they stop, pull out, and horn often.

## Buses
- The public buses read as **red** with a lighter band.
- Private buses are more varied (blue/white, multicolour stripes, roof racks).
- In the game:
  - no operator logos or exact liveries;
  - colours and proportions only.

## Tuk traffic
- Three-wheelers everywhere, in black, green, red, blue, cream and maroon, often with personalised trim.
- The hero tuk is **deep teal/turquoise with yellow accents**, an original design that is not a copy of any manufacturer's body.

## Pettah shops
- Narrow, deep shop-houses of two to four storeys:
  - roller shutters, goods pushed out to the shop line;
  - upper floors with balconies, faded paint and AC units;
  - wall-mounted shop boards with names in up to three scripts.
- Trades cluster: textiles, hardware, electrical, spices, plastics, bags.

## Market spillover
- Trade spills onto the pavement:
  - umbrellas and canopies in saturated colours;
  - stacked sacks and crates, hanging goods, plastic stools;
  - handcarts parked half on the kerb.
- Pedestrians walk in the road edge because the pavement is full. The Market Approach gets the highest pedestrian density, and buses are rare there (`bus_spawn_scale` 0.15).

## Multilingual signage
- Sinhala, Tamil and English share boards.
- The game renders all three with Noto Sans, Noto Sans Sinhala and Noto Sans Tamil (SIL OFL).
- Native-speaker review of the strings is still required before release.

## Handcarts
- Flat wooden carts with two large spoked wheels and long handles, loaded with sacks or boxes.
- Parked at the pavement edge or pushed slowly along the kerb.

## Utility clutter
- Concrete poles with drooping bundles of wires crossing the street, pole-mounted transformers, street lamps, and loose cables between buildings.

## Tropical lighting
- Late morning: a high, hard sun, short dark shadows, a pale hazy sky and slightly washed-out distance.
- Colour is strongest in the shade (awnings, umbrellas) and faded on sun-baked walls.
